The idea

For years, African television has been losing the living room to the phone in your hand. International streamers arrived with paywalls. Local ones fought for the same scarce paid hours. And all the while, millions of people simply found another way to watch for free.

We do not think the answer is another subscription. It is a channel: a curated, always-on experience, paid for by advertising, as easy as pressing play.

Television packed up. The audience didn’t.

Paid platforms asked people to subscribe. Pirate sites asked nothing at all.

YouTube proved the other model: the ads pay, the people watch.

We bring back the joy of just tuning in.

Investors

The model that already works, built as a channel.

  • The living room moved into the pocket Traditional television in Africa has been losing hours to phones and tablets. Stations have struggled to keep the lights on as attention walked away from the big screen.
  • Subscriptions ran into a hard truth Netflix and other platforms arrived. African services emerged too — Showmax among them, and later shut down. They were competing for paid watch-time from an audience that would often rather download a film than pay a monthly fee. Piracy was not a side issue. It was the alternative product.
  • YouTube already settled the argument The audience watches for free. Advertising pays. Creators are rewarded. That is the economic logic we are building around — not another paywall.
  • Curation is the missing piece Television’s great gift was that it took the stress away. You did not have to decide. Yellow Popcorn brings that curated, 24-hour experience into the digital ecosystem, first on YouTube, then onto terrestrial networks.
